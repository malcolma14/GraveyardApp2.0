# GraveyardApp 2.0 — Richest Person in the Graveyard

A single-page, dependency-free web experience that walks you through a moonlit
graveyard of history's wealthiest people. Every resident is ranked on "The
Final Leaderboard" by their peak (inflation-adjusted) fortune — and every one
of them took exactly $0 with them.

The title comes from the Steve Jobs line the page closes on:

> "Being the richest man in the cemetery doesn't matter to me. Going to bed at
> night saying we've done something wonderful — that's what matters to me."

## Features

- **The Final Leaderboard** — all residents ranked by fortune at peak, with a
  "took: $0" column for perspective.
- **Walk the Grounds** — an interactive graveyard of CSS tombstones; click any
  stone (or its leaderboard row) for the resident's story, fortune, and epitaph.
- **Atmosphere** — animated starfield, glowing moon, and drifting ground fog,
  all in pure CSS/vanilla JS. No frameworks, no build step, no network calls.

## Running it

Open `Richest Person in the Graveyard.html` in any modern browser. That's it.

```sh
# or serve it locally
python3 -m http.server
```

## Residents

Mansa Musa, Jakob Fugger, John D. Rockefeller, Andrew Carnegie, Henry Ford,
Cornelius Vanderbilt, Sam Walton, and Howard Hughes. Fortune figures are
popular inflation-adjusted estimates — historians disagree on the numbers,
but agree on the ending.
